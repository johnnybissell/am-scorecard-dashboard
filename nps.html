Quarter-over-quarter written analysis

Written synthesis of detractor feedback patterns each quarter, cross-referenced by TCA tier, lead volume, and practice area.

2025 Q1NPS +38.197 responses · 17 detractor comments
The highest-performing quarter. Complaints were diffuse — ease of use, product gaps, bugs, and support access each under 25%. No systemic failure had taken hold. Pattern points to individual friction rather than structural problems.
By TCA
Detractors concentrated in <$1K TCA (10 of 17). Complaints lean toward product gaps and UX. The $1K–$3K tier cited support access more.
By lead volume
0-lead firms complained most about support access. 25–74 lead firms hit onboarding friction. High-volume (200+) firms were quiet.
By practice area
Low detractor counts across all practices. Estate Planning had the most (2). Business Law flagged bugs and support. No red flags this quarter.
Top signal
“The core functionality hasn't changed in six years... Marketing tracking is still not good.” A product staleness complaint that grew louder in subsequent quarters.

Product gaps were the top complaint across every lead volume tier. The 25–74 lead tier had the highest concentration.
By practice area
Estate Planning emerged as the largest detractor source (11). Business Law NPS hit −60, its lowest point.
Top signal

Cross-cutting patterns
What the full arc tells you

The <$1K TCA cohort is your NPS floor. 67% of all responses, disproportionately driving every negative theme. Their complaint profile has been consistent for six quarters: product gaps, UX friction, and inability to get help.

High-volume firms (200+ leads) are your most important bellwether. When they complain about bugs it signals a reliability event. When they shift to product gaps it signals feature ceiling.

Criminal Defense is a practice-area emergency. NPS went from +77 (Q2 2025) to −50 (Q2 2026) in four quarters. Worth a dedicated outreach campaign.

Onboarding is a persistent low-level signal that periodically produces high-severity individual cases (~3-4% of all responses). The firms it hits score 0-2 and feel they wasted money. Direct outreach to those accounts is warranted; a broader program overhaul is not.

Pricing as a complaint is new and worth watching. Hit 15% in Q2 2026. Concentrated in <$1K TCA accounts questioning the bill heading into renewal.
